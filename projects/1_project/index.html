RINSE: Smoothness-Driven Data Quality for Imitation Learning

Submitted to IROS 2026 — UCLA Robot Intelligence Lab

RINSE is a framework for evaluating and filtering demonstration quality in imitation learning based on trajectory smoothness. Grounded in motor control theory—where smoothness is a hallmark of skilled execution—the framework introduces two complementary metrics that operate without policy training, rollouts, or dataset-global statistics.

Motivation

Behavioral cloning (BC) is fundamentally limited by demonstration quality. Real-world datasets contain trajectories of varying quality due to operator skill differences, teleoperation artifacts, and procedural inconsistencies. Standard BC treats all demonstrations equally, but the policy loss decomposes as:

\[ \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_s\!\left[\|\pi_\theta(s) - \mu^*(s)\|^2\right] + \mathbb{E}_s\!\left[\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\mathrm{Var}[a \mid s]\right)\right] \]

The second term is a noise floor \(\bar{\sigma}^2\) that cannot be reduced by training—only by curating the data. With the BC regret bound \(J(\hat{\pi}) - J(\pi^*) \leq H^2 \varepsilon\), even modest reductions in per-step error amplify into substantial rollout improvements through the \(H^2\) compounding factor.

Metrics

Spectral Arc Length (SAL)

Adapted from rehabilitation science, SAL quantifies smoothness in the frequency domain. Given a speed signal \(v[0{:}T{-}1]\), the one-sided FFT yields normalized magnitudes \(\hat{V}_k\), and the arc length of this spectral curve gives:

\[ \mathrm{SAL}(v) := -\sum_{k=1}^{T/2} \sqrt{\left(f_k - f_{k-1}\right)^2 + \left(\hat{V}_k - \hat{V}_{k-1}\right)^2} \]

Less-negative SAL (closer to zero) indicates smoother motion. The metric is scale-invariant, computable in \(\mathcal{O}(T \log T)\), and has demonstrated ICC > 0.9 in rehabilitation benchmarks.

Trajectory-Envelope Distance (TED)

TED captures phase-aware geometric coherence. Each trajectory is partitioned by gripper contact into dense boundary regions and sparse interior segments. A low-control-point Bézier curve is fit via greedy refinement, and the normalized DTW distance between the raw trajectory and its smooth envelope yields the TED score:

\[ d_{\mathrm{TED}}(\tau) = \frac{1}{|P|}\sum_{(t,s)\in P} \sqrt{\|x_t - \tilde{x}_s\|^2 + w_{\mathrm{ori}}^2\,\theta(R_t, \tilde{R}_s)^2} \]

Lower TED indicates higher intrinsic smoothness. The contact-aware partition avoids penalizing natural discontinuities at grasp/release transitions.

On the real-world Push Block task, TED-filtered subsets at 50% data consistently outperformed random subsets by 16–32 percentage points. Filtered top-50 demos on RoboMimic Square converged to 75% success in ~40k steps vs ~450k for the full 300 demos (>10× speed-up).

Complementarity

SAL excels at detecting free-space jitter (e.g., Transport task), while TED captures spatial artifacts near contact (e.g., Square task). Together they provide comprehensive trajectory quality assessment across different task structures.
